# Changelog

## 2.1.0

### Changed

- **Delay from atspm's Queue Model.** When `approach_delay` has `Queue_Delay_Seconds` (atspm 3.1 with `queue_model=True`), delay per vehicle is `SUM(Queue_Delay_Seconds) / SUM(Total_Actuations)`: the Sudden Rise in Delay check, the detector triage's Impact and the coordination impact chart. A row where it is NULL, or a table without it, takes `Delay_Seconds + Queued_Delay_Seconds` as before.

## 2.0.0 (2026-11-19)

### Breaking

- **The old Pedestrian section is gone**, with its `pedestrian` alert type. It flagged a day when a phase's walks went up while its presses per walk went down, from daily totals; Pedestrian Button Health finds the same faults per button from the raw events (held ON, walks with no presses) and tells a ped recall apart from a fault. `generate()` no longer returns `alerts['pedestrian']` or `hourly_data['ped_hourly']`, never reads or writes its history file (`past_pedestrian_alerts.parquet` is left as it is), and `generate_pdf_report()` lost `filtered_df_ped`, `ped_hourly_df`, `ped_figures` and `ongoing_ped_figures`. The `pedestrian` input stays: the detector triage and the ped charts read it.
- **Detector wording**: a detector with no counts is 'Silent'; 'Counts fall' only where some counts remain, everywhere in the report.
- **Alarms count once**: atspm writes an open alarm at once and again when it ends, with the same start; the report counts it once.
- **Python 3.12+, DuckDB 2.0+, pandas 3.1+.** Tested on Python 3.12 to 3.15. Ibis is gone: inputs are pandas DataFrames or DuckDB relations (`con.table(...)`, `con.sql(...)`). For an Ibis table pass `ibis_table.to_pandas()`.
- **The traffic-anomaly detector section is gone**, with its `actuations` alert type. Detector health now comes from [atspm-detector](https://pypi.org/project/atspm-detector/)'s `health()` on raw events: new inputs `atspm_detector_findings` and `atspm_detector_minutes`, the `run_atspm_detector_findings` helper and the `[detector]` extra.
- **`generate(detector_health=...)` is now `generate(detector_counts=...)`.** It is only the 15-minute detector counts (`TimeStamp`, `DeviceId`, `Detector`, `Total`, e.g. atspm's `actuations` table) for the days the detector health covers, drawn in the detector health charts. The `prediction` and `anomaly` columns are no longer read.
- `generate_pdf_report()` lost the unused `save_to_disk`, `clearance_yellow_min_seconds` and `clearance_red_min_seconds` arguments, and `create_phase_skip_plots()` the unused `skip_multiplier` and `assumed_cycle_length`.

- **Detector health reads one table.** `atspm_detector_findings` (`DeviceId`, `DetectorType`, `Detector`, `Start`, `End`, `Label`, `Confidence`, `Detail`) holds vehicle and ped together, one row per finding. Stored minutes hold `DetectorType` and `SuspectMinutes`, and alert history holds `DetectorType`; files from before read as vehicle.

### Changed

- **Coordination groups from a corridor table.** New optional signals column `corridor`: signals with the same value are a group, a value no other signal has means the signal runs on its own and is never judged, and signals without a value are grouped from what they run, only with each other.
- **A group is no more than half a mile between signals.** Learned groups are chains of signals that run together, each link no longer than half a mile; a signal in line between two of them, within half a mile of both, links them. A signal in line between two signals that run together, within half a mile of the nearer, is added by position even where they are in two chains.
- **Coordination is shown by group, with no table.** Each group's alerts are shown together: the alerted signals' names, a map of the signals around it (plain road base map, left out when it cannot be fetched; no map without coordinates) and a chart of cycle length by time of day for each distinct day of the last 7 that shows a signal running something else than its group for 30 minutes or more. Nothing is shaded. The clock chart's title says what was found. `coordination_figures` (5) now counts groups per report; a group not shown is not filed as told. Alert columns `ChartDay`, `ChartOwn` and `ChartGroup` are now `ChartMembers`, `ChartWeek` and `ChartNoData`; `prepare_coordination_table` and `in_report_order` are gone; new `group_rows`, `whole_group` and `CoordinationGroup`. The impact chart is no longer drawn in the report (`create_coordination_impact_chart` is kept).
- **Shorter tables.** Detector Health Data Only lists 10 signals per report (`data_only_table_signals`), each phase skip table 5 (`phase_skip_table_signals`), Controller Alarms the 10 signals with the most alarms over six weeks (`alarm_table_signals`, was every signal by name). Alerts a table leaves out are not filed as told, so they come up on later runs.
- **Ongoing tables take turns** (`ongoing_rotation_enabled`, on): each shows the ongoing alerts that have waited longest, weighted by how bad they are, so every alert comes round and worse ones more often; a table keeps its row limit, a signal's rows staying together. New input `generate(ongoing_last_shown=...)` and result `updated_ongoing_last_shown` keep when each was last shown. A table's total counts the alerts waiting their turn.
- **Max-out drain.** New `maxout_drain_enabled` (on by default): a max-out alert must also still be high at the end of its day. An hourly running total adds each hour's max-out share above that hour's usual share and takes off `maxout_drain_per_hour` (0.1); it must end the day above `maxout_drain_threshold` (1.0). A spike that went back to normal drains away. Flagged days get `MaxOutEndTotal`.
- New option `preempt_false_calls_enabled` (on by default): a **False Calls** alert for an emergency vehicle preempt input with most of its calls at the shortest call length, not seen at nearby signals (1 or more a day, at least half the input's calls, at most 30% seen nearby; with no nearby signal to check against, 1.5 or more a day and at least 80%). It takes the place of an Increase on the same input. An Increase whose calls nearby signals saw is real traffic and is no longer reported. The report names other signals only where the input has linked preempts. The preempt charts shade the usual call lengths, and a chart with one alert lets its text run onto a second line instead of cutting it off.
- **Emergency Vehicle Preempt, Transit Signal Priority and Rail are three sections**, in that order, after Pedestrian Button Health and before the missing data sections, instead of one Preempt Monitoring section after Controller Alarms. Each preempt call is classed by its episode: a constant call (5 s or more) is an emergency vehicle; a burst of pulses (under 1.5 s) on a preempt input is a bus priority signal read as preempt calls when TSP check-ins (112) come with the input's bursts, and chatter on an emergency vehicle input when they do not; rail by `preempt_rail_numbers`. A controller set up for TSP logs a bus as TSP events only, with no preempt call, so normal TSP is never among the calls. New optional input `preempt_events` (raw 102/104, 105 entry and 112 TSP check-in); without it every burst is chatter. The emergency vehicle checks (frequency, chatter, stuck on, missed vehicles, cascades) read emergency vehicle calls only. New TSP alerts for an input with 5 or more bus calls in 7 days: Ran As Preempt when that many ran as full preempts, else Priority Interrupted (each pulse cancels the bus's priority request and starts it again). Stored calls and daily counts carry `Class`; counts and alert history from before read as they did (emergency vehicle, or rail by input). Preempt alert history is keyed on `Class` too, so an old Chatter does not hold back the new TSP alert. Signal Focus names the kind ("Emergency vehicle preempt 1", "Transit Signal Priority on preempt input 3", "Rail preempt 5") and the overview's preempt rows split emergency and bus calls.
- **Ongoing Issues is one section at the end of the report**, after every new alert, instead of an Ongoing section after each check. Each check's ongoing table and charts are a subsection named after the check, in the checks' order.
- **Missing data by hour.** The missing data table gives When Missing (the hours with any record missing, newest first, three at most and the rest counted), Hours Missing and an hourly sparkline over the last 7 days, instead of the worst day and its share; its chart is hourly. System outages give When (the hours the region averaged 30% or more missing) and Hours Out instead of Days and the worst day's Missing Data %. Which signals and days alert does not change: both checks still decide by day. New results `hourly_data['missing_hourly']` and `['outage_hourly']`.
- **Signal Focus overview chart.** Each signal's charts open with one overview that lines up its detector and button faults with the percent max out, average phase wait and greens of the phases its table names (hour by hour, against their normal range), its skips, preempt calls and hours without data, on the same days as its detector charts. It replaces the separate wait, max out, greens, skip, preempt and missing data charts there, which had different time ranges. When every known vehicle detector of a signal was down at once (held ON or silent, spare channels that counted nothing left out), its table says so in one line with when, the overview has one row for them all, and no detector chart follows. A measured rise in phase wait or max-outs reads "Phase wait rose on phases 2, 3 & 5", or "Phase wait rose on all phases" when those are every phase the signal ran; the numbers stay when it has no chart. The measures are plain lines through the middle of each hour over a smoothed normal range, percent max out always tops out at 100%, a fault with no set time shows as another fault (not shaded behind the measures), a long stretch with no wait while the signal sent data says no calls were logged, and a preempt row with no calls in these days says so, with the day of its last call. `create_phase_measure_chart` is gone.
- **Ped charts show the share of cycles with a walk**, hour by hour, instead of walks per 15 minutes: a phase on ped recall sits at 100% and one walking on presses below it, whatever the cycle length. Cycles are the greens of the signal's busiest phase, the one on recall, which also counts the cycles of a signal running free. New `create_atspm_detector_health_plots(ped_cycles=...)`.
- The Pedestrian Button Health heading no longer has a paragraph under it.
- **No explanation paragraphs.** Every section and subsection heading sits straight over its table: no paragraph under the headings (Signal Focus, each check, the preempt sections, Ongoing Issues, the information tables) and no note at the top about recurring issues. The data-stopped warning stays.
- A heading goes to the next page unless the start of its table fits under it.
- **Two running totals.** Bad findings (Confidence 0.8 or more) keep the old settings: 30 minutes a day forgiven, alert over 120, capped at 180. Suspect findings (0.5 to under 0.8) add up more slowly: 15 forgiven, a day counts at most 90 for a detector and 120 for a ped button, alert over 240, capped at 270, so one suspect day never alerts and several days to a week can. A single run's 3-day window cannot alert a detector by itself. A fault alert's status says which total went over. A finding with no time of its own (busy at night, misses vehicles at night) is reported at once at Confidence 0.8 or more, and under that each day it holds adds to the suspect total.
- **Every fault counts its real minutes** toward the running totals. Stuck-on holds, ped long holds, spiky counts and count zones held ON are no longer scaled (a 47-minute ped hold used to count as 562 minutes and alert at once).
- A count drop's text gives the first hour judged low as its start, the same as its first listed period. A finding over whole days names no such hour and keeps the time in its text.
- **Health tables have one narrative column.** Details and Fault Periods are now What Is Wrong (What Happened in the information tables): what is wrong and how bad first, exact times only when there are few, repeated periods grouped ("Held ON 19 times, 24 h in total, mostly nights, Oct 30 to Nov 1"), with a length limit. Alerts carry a new `Evidence` column for it; `prepare_atspm_detector_health_table(periods_header=...)` is now `story_header`.
- **Health tables read shorter.** The status column is gone (being listed means there is a problem) and Det reads "Det 22, Ph8 Count, 1 lane", without the function Other; Button reads "Button 4". The vehicle fault tables gain an Hourly Actuations sparkline, from `generate_pdf_report(atspm_detector_hourly=...)`. The Detector Health heading no longer has a paragraph under it.
- Ped health charts: walks in a panel under the presses (the share of cycles with a walk), those of the flagged buttons' phases in the buttons' colours, the signal's other phases in grey. A stuck period with brief presses in it shows them.
- Health charts: larger text, a caption too long for one line wraps instead of being cut, the legend moved above the plot, and lines at zero are drawn over the axis line. Sparklines keep their lowest and highest values at full thickness.
- **System outages are one row per real outage.** Days in a row are one outage and regions out on the same days are one row. A day that recovered ends an outage, so the next one is new, not a repeat of any outage in the last 21 days. Ongoing gives the day it began and its length in days, or the day it ended.
- Missing data expects 92 fifteen-minute records instead of 96 on the first Sunday of November, whose repeated 1:00-2:00 am is left out of the data.

### Added

- **atspm's per-phase measures as optional inputs**: `split_failures`, `arrival_on_green`, `platoon_ratio` and `approach_delay` (DataFrames or DuckDB relations, filtered to the signals and days needed before reading). No measure is used from a detector while it is faulting: hours whose stop-bar zones (split failures) or advance zones (the other three) had a known fault are left out everywhere. Without them the report is as before.
- **Delay per vehicle in the detector triage's Impact**: a harmed phase with enough arrivals is told by its delay per vehicle instead of its phase wait; never from its faulty advance zone. The tier is still judged on phase wait.
- **Coordination impact chart** after each coordination alert's chart: arrivals on green and delay per vehicle by hour, the out-of-step days against the days in step (or its weekdays), the group's median and its nearest signal; only with enough data on both sides and something to see.
- New sections, each off by default with its own switch and alert type: **Phases Ending Green Too Early** (`phase_timing`: split failures without max outs, naming both causes: a gap time too short, or a stop-bar zone not extending the green), **Sudden Rise in Delay** (`delay_rise`), **Rising Split Failures** (`split_failure_trend`) and **Falling Platoon Ratio** (`platoon_ratio_trend`, the max-out method; silent until 18 days of data). None counts toward Signal Focus.

- **Coordination Plans and Clocks** section, new alert type `coordination` (keyed on `DeviceId` and `Cause`), one chart per alert. **Clock** (`coordination_clock_check_enabled`, on): a signal whose emergency vehicle calls line up with nearby signals' calls an hour apart since a clock change it missed, or some other fixed time apart, read from the stored preempt calls. Over ODOT's six weeks to 11-15 it flagged one signal, 2B095, which did not fall back on 11-01, from the 11-08 report on. **Plan** (`coordination_plan_check_enabled`, off): signals left without a group are added to the group whose end signal they run with part of the day (03021) or, behind `coordination_position_check_enabled` (off), that they sit in the middle of (2B418); alerts carry a `Confidence` (High inside a group, Check at its end), only High ones go on to the Ongoing list, and a signal with a clock alert gets no plan alert; a signal that runs another plan, runs free or changes plans at other hours than the coordinated signals around it, on weekdays, Saturdays or Sundays, repeating; needs `coordination_agg` with `Pattern` over six weeks. The phase skip charts then draw only the span `phase_wait` covers. A clock check from the daily traffic curve was tried and left out: compared with nearby signals on one day it gave about six false signals a day; before against after the 11-01 change, 22 signals' curves moved half an hour or more, and 2B095 stood alone only when also asking for a close match (correlation 0.9) and the direction a missed change gives, tested on that one change.
- **A stopped feed is told, not counted.** When most signals' data ends together short of the expected end of the day, the report says "Data stopped updating at ..." at its top, and nothing after that time is missing data, an outage or a detector fault; a fault running into that time reads "until the data stopped". New `generate(expected_data_end=...)` and result `data_stopped_at`.
- Health tables say things once: within a signal, equal neighbours in a column are one merged cell, and in What Is Wrong a detector shows only what the one before it does not say.
- Vehicle health sparklines draw the detector's usual count for the hour (`detector_usual_counts`) as a thin grey line. A detector with under 5 samples of a typical quarter hour has none.
- **Signal Focus section**, first in each report after the joke: at most 3 signals with new problems in more than one area (`signal_focus_signals`, 0 leaves it out), one at a time: a subtitle, a two-column table of problem type and measured problems, one a line, and its charts in the table's order (at most `signal_focus_charts_per_signal`, default 24), every phase a line names in colour. The score counts new alerts only, weighted to the most recent day of data: severity of each problem, with clearance weighed by its share of samples, plus a point for each area and two for each pair of problems that belong together. Missing data is listed last and never counts. A detector or button fault measured to affect operations counts as two areas (the detection, and how the signal ran), so harm to three or more phases can qualify with nothing else, and a ped button's harm scores a point for each further phase hurt as a detector's does. A signal needs a score of 7, a bar set for one to three signals over all regions on an ordinary day, from problems that belong together (`signal_focus_related_only`, on by default): a group that shares a phase (a detector or button fault and max outs, skips or another fault on its phase or a phase it hurt) or a flash alarm and a conflict has to span two areas and score 7 by itself, and problems that only happen at the same signal (a detector fault and a preempt input) qualify only when there are 3 or more different kinds of them. A selected signal's preempt alerts bring the preempt section's charts with them; each report lists its own top 3. One event's measured effect is told once, for its detectors and buttons together. Ongoing issues of a selected signal are told, marked as ongoing. A signal selected there leaves every other table and chart of that report; the alert tables `generate()` returns and the history keep it. New result `signal_focus`.
- **Detector triage** (`detector_triage_enabled`, default off): Detector Health lists vehicle detectors and ped buttons by urgency, in two tables. Affecting Operations: service lost (its own phase gets far fewer greens or walks), then harm to other phases (they wait longer or max out more, weighted by their traffic), with an Impact column that names the phases affected ("Phase wait on other phases up 20 s on average over 4 h: Ph4 20 to 40 s": phase wait is the time from a phase's call to its green, a measure of the phases, not a vehicle delay), and the charts. Data Only: the faults with no measured effect, and those not measured, with no charts. Max outs on the detector's own phase no longer rank. It compares the same clock hours on other days, from `terminations`, `phase_wait`, `has_data`, `pedestrian` and `atspm_detector_config`, with the new inputs `atspm_ped_inputs` (the phase each button calls) and `detector_usual_counts` (traffic weights). It never changes which detectors alert. The history records what the two tables listed. New result `silent_ped_phases`: phases with no press at all that normally get many, for review.
- **Harm to other phases is a Bayes test, not a fixed rise.** A rise in wait or max outs during a fault counts when the fault is its likelier cause: the fault days are weighed against the spread of the normal days in the same hours, against unusual days like the ones those had, and against something else having changed for all of them, starting from how likely that kind of fault is to hurt other phases and whether it made its own phase run more. A rise of 3 s can count when the normal days agree closely (it was 5 s for all); a larger one does not when they are few or scattered. Faults of one signal with much the same times are judged once and read as one row; a fault is judged on the hours it had to itself when it has enough of them. New triage columns `HarmProbability` and `Group`, new `detector_triage.effect_probability()`, and `prepare_atspm_detector_health_table(impact=...)` for the two tables.
- `table_row_limits`: a table's own row limit instead of `max_table_rows`.
- Sparklines on the ped button, set-up, lane closure and timing plan tables.
- **Pedestrian Button Health section** in the PDF, laid out like Detector Health: faults, charts of presses (`ped_press_counts`), set-up issues as information, and an ongoing section. It replaces the old Pedestrian section (see Breaking).
- `atspm_detector_config`: the detector table the health run read. The findings table names no phase, so this keeps "Ph6 Advance" in the tables and the charts grouped by phase.
- Preempt cascade review: preempt alerts are checked against the signals up and down the road. New input `preempt_calls` and results `updated_preempt_calls`, `preempt_cascade_links` and `overruled_preempt_alerts`.
- `updated_atspm_detector_minutes` in the result.

### Fixed

- **Ongoing table turns on pandas 3**: the first run (nothing shown yet) and the row limit no longer fail; `Timestamp.dayofweek` (deprecated) is now `day_of_week` throughout.
- Tables with many columns no longer shrink the trend column under its sparkline or run headers into each other: the trend keeps its width, long headers wrap and the signal name gives way first.
- Clearance intervals no longer fail with "incompatible merge keys ... datetime64[ns] and datetime64[us]" when timeline times are microseconds (as DuckDB returns them) and an end time has to be filled from the duration, or on pandas 3.

### Faster (same output)

Every alert table, the hourly data, the phase skip tables and the table data behind the PDFs are identical to before, and the PDFs are byte-identical apart from their creation timestamps. On a 60-signal, 21-day sample `generate()` (with the PDFs) runs in about half the time.

- Charts are drawn without pyplot, so an interactive backend (Tk on Windows) no longer opens a hidden window for every chart and sparkline.
- A chart that appears both in its region's report and in All Regions is drawn and rendered to PNG once; repeated sparklines are rendered once.
- The overlap dual indication and the three signal conflict checks share one DuckDB connection and build the report day's clean indications once, instead of once per check.
- Clearance intervals: rows the check never reads (valid greens, phase calls, detector and pedestrian intervals) are dropped first, movements that cannot alert are set aside before the per-movement loop, and the stop-time overlap is found for all intervals in one pass.
- Preempt cascades find each neighbouring call with an as-of join instead of pairing every call with every neighbouring call (about 9x faster on the whole network's stored calls).
- Already-text `DeviceId` columns are no longer copied and converted.
